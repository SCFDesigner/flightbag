    Two lessons, one flight

    1. Ground (discuss): weather, IFR cross-country plan & alternate, checklists, de-/anti-icing, attitude-instrument fundamentals (pitch · bank · power), positive exchange.
    2. Taxi: instrument cockpit check — narrate it on the first turn.
    3. Departure climb: constant-airspeed climb (cruise climb).
    4. To KAFW: S&L, standard-rate turns on vectors, airspeed change cruise → 90 for the approach; the ILS glideslope is a constant-rate descent. Missed → hold.
    5. KLUD RNAV, partial panel: timed turns with the HI covered; circle (daylight preferred).
    6. Airwork block: steep turns L/R → compass turns to N & S → constant-rate climb/descent → airspeed change in a turn → unusual attitudes, full then partial panel.
    7. KDTO ILS 18L (you teach it), then night full-stop landings.

    KAFW runways are 16L/34R and 16R/34L — there is no 36R there (36R is KDTO).

    Ground — discuss

    Weather InformationFL7Discuss

    Know

    • 91.103: all available information. Standard briefing (1800wxbrief) + AWC: GFA, G-AIRMETs (Sierra = IFR/mtn obscuration, Tango = turbulence/LLWS, Zulu = icing & freezing level), SIGMETs/convective SIGMETs, PIREPs.
    • METAR/TAF for departure, destination and alternate (KDTO, KAFW, KLUD) — TAF timing vs. ETA drives the 1-2-3 decision.
    • Winds/temps aloft, freezing level (feeds the icing talk), NOTAMs for the ILS/GPS approaches you plan to fly.
    • Night: temperature/dew-point spread closes after sunset — watch for fog and low stratus on the way home.

    Teach

    • Big picture → hazards → route → destination, then a go/no-go against personal minimums.

    Common errors

    • METAR-only briefing; ignoring TAF timing; missing approach/NAVAID NOTAMs.

    USAA p18 · 14 CFR 91.103

    IFR Cross-Country PlanningFL7Discuss

    Know

    • Alternate (91.169): required unless the destination, 1 hr before to 1 hr after ETA, forecasts ceiling ≥ 2,000 ft and visibility ≥ 3 SM (1-2-3). Standard alternate minimums: 600-2 precision, 800-2 non-precision.
    • Fuel (91.167): to destination, then alternate, then 45 min at normal cruise.
    • Clearance (CRAFT): Clearance limit, Route, Altitude, Frequency, Transponder. File to KAFW, practice approaches; pick up a practice clearance back to KDTO.
    • Lost comms (91.185): route AVE-F; altitude = highest of MEA, expected, assigned.

    Teach

    • Student builds the plan; you probe: "Why that alternate? What if the glideslope is out of service?"

    Common errors

    • No alternate or fuel analysis; filing below the MEA; no lost-comms plan; read-back errors on the clearance (USAA p19).

    USAA p18–19 · 14 CFR 91.167, 91.169, 91.185

    Use of ChecklistsFL7Discuss

    Know

    • Read-and-do (each item done as it's read — abnormal/emergency, unfamiliar phases) vs. flow, then verify (normal phases).
    • Why: memory fails under workload, fatigue and interruption — single-pilot IFR at night has all three.
    • Approach phase: in-range briefing before the approach (USAA p56).

    Teach

    • Model it: read, touch, verify aloud. After an interruption, restart that section.

    Common errors

    • Reciting without looking; skipping items when rushed; checklist "from memory".

    USAA p56

    Instrument Cockpit CheckFL7Taxi

    Know

    • Before start: VOR check within 30 days (91.171 — VOT/ground checkpoint ±4°, airborne ±6°, dual ±4° between receivers); altimeter/static (91.411) and transponder (91.413) within 24 calendar months; GPS database current, RAIM/WAAS.
    • After start: altimeter within ±75 ft of field elevation · COM/NAV set and identified · HI set · GPS loaded for the route · VOR/OBS checks.
    • Taxi: compass swings freely, full of fluid · ASI 0 · AI erect within 5 min, ≤ 5° bank in taxi turns · TC shows the turn, ball to the outside, no flag · HI turns correctly and agrees with the compass · VSI 0 (or note the offset as your zero).

    Teach

    • Call it out on the first taxi turn: "Turning left — needle left, ball right, heading decreasing, AI steady."

    Common errors

    • Rushing or skipping checks; not verifying database/RAIM; seeing an abnormal indication and taking off anyway.

    USAA p20–21 · 14 CFR 91.171, 91.411, 91.413

    Positive Exchange of ControlsFL7Discuss

    Know

    • Three steps: "You have the flight controls." → "I have the flight controls." → "You have the flight controls." Plus a visual check that the other pilot has them.
    • Exactly one pilot is flying at all times. Brief it before engine start.

    Teach

    • Rehearse it before unusual attitudes — the recovery starts on "You have the flight controls."

    Common errors

    • "Got it?"; both or neither pilot on the controls; taking the controls without announcing.

    AFH (FAA-H-8083-3) ch. 1

    Aircraft De-icing / Anti-icingFL7Discuss

    Know

    • Anti-ice prevents formation (pitot heat, carb heat, heated windshield, TKS, Type IV ground fluid). De-ice removes ice after it forms (boots, Type I ground fluid).
    • Our Cessnas: pitot heat, carb heat (carbureted) or alternate air (fuel-injected), cabin defrost, alternate static source — not approved for flight into known icing.
    • IPH App. A: turn anti-ice on before entering visible moisture at +5 °C or colder; ice is hard to see at night — use a flashlight. Get out: climb to colder air, descend to above-freezing air, leave visible moisture, or turn toward known ice-free air; consider landing.
    • No takeoff with frost, ice or snow adhering to wings, tail or control surfaces.
    • Tailplane stall cues (often at flap extension): pitch buffet, light elevator, sudden nose-down → flaps back to the previous setting, back pressure, reduce power.

    Teach

    • Ask when they'd turn pitot heat on tonight; tie it to the freezing level from the weather brief.

    Common errors

    • Waiting to see ice before using pitot heat; pressing on "to see if it gets better"; not watching OAT.

    IPH (FAA-H-8083-16B) App. A · AC 91-74B

    Attitude Instrument FundamentalsFL7Discuss

    Know

    • Three skills: cross-check, interpretation, control. Scan patterns: selected radial (AI hub), inverted-V, rectangular. Scan errors: fixation, omission, emphasis.
    • Control & performance: set attitude (AI) and power (tach/MP) → check performance (ALT, VSI, ASI, HI, TC). Primary & supporting: which instrument gives the most pertinent pitch/bank/power information right now.
    • In any transition the AI is primary for pitch and bank (USAA p25, p29).
    PrimaryPitchBankPower

    Std-rate turnALTTCASI

    Pitch control

    • AI sets it; ALT/VSI/ASI confirm it. Altitude off → VSI about twice the error (USAA caps it at 200 fpm); under 100 ft use a ½-bar change, over 100 ft a full bar. Trim after every change.

    Bank control

    • Bank no more than the degrees to turn, never more than standard rate. HI primary in straight flight, TC in standard-rate turns; ball centred with rudder.

    Power control

    • 100 RPM ≈ 5 kt ≈ 100 fpm. Use the power table instead of fishing; tach/MP is primary while power is changing (USAA p28).

    Common errors

    • Fixation, omission, emphasis; over-controlling; chasing the VSI (it lags); not trimming.

    USAA p23–30

    Fly — by reference to instruments

    Straight-and-Level FlightFL6·7Fly

    Procedure

    • Pitch 0° on the AI → set power (172: 2300 RPM ≈ 110 kt) → trim → hold with primary/supporting instruments → include the engine gauges → reset the HI to the compass every 15 min.

    Teach

    • Hands-off trim test. Show that a ½-bar pitch change gives a steady, small climb or descent.

    Common errors

    • Fixation; chasing the VSI; not trimming; fixing altitude while the heading wanders.

    ±100 ft · ±10° · ±10 kt · bank ±5°

    USAA p23–24

    Standard-Rate TurnsFL6·7Fly

    Procedure

    • Coordinated roll-in, AI to the approximate bank (≈ 15% of airspeed — 110 kt ≈ 16°), TC confirms standard rate.
    • Back pressure / pitch and power as needed to hold altitude and airspeed.
    • Lead the rollout by ½ the bank; AI during rollout, HI to stop on the heading; reset level pitch and power.

    Common errors

    • Losing altitude in the turn; ballooning on rollout; overbanking; skidding (ball); overshooting the heading.

    ±100 ft · ±10 kt · bank ±5° · rollout ±10°

    USAA p31–32

    Change of Airspeed (level & turning)FL6·7Fly

    Procedure

    • Power → Pitch → Trim. About 100 RPM per 5 kt (172: 110 → 90 kt is 2300 → 2000 RPM).
    • Raise the nose as it slows (lower it as it speeds up) to hold altitude.
    • In a turn, reduce bank as you slow to stay at standard rate (110 kt ≈ 16.5°, 90 kt ≈ 13.5°).
    • Tach/MP is primary while the power changes (USAA p28).

    Teach

    • Level flight first, then the same thing in a standard-rate turn — the only new step is the bank.

    Common errors

    • Altitude excursion when power changes; forgetting the bank adjustment; no rudder for the yaw; not trimming.

    ±100 ft · ±10° · ±10 kt · bank ±5°

    USAA p27–28 · power p6, p8

    Constant-Airspeed Climbs & DescentsFL6·7Fly

    Procedure

    • Power first, then pitch to the attitude for the airspeed; trim.
    • AI primary during the transition, then ASI primary for pitch, tach for power.
    • 172: cruise climb 90 kt, full power, +5° (≈ +500 fpm) · Vy 76 kt, +7° (≈ +800) · cruise descent 100 kt, 2100 RPM, −2° (≈ −500).
    • Level off by leading 10% of the VSI (500 fpm → 50 ft); after a climb, hold climb power until cruise speed, then set cruise power.

    Common errors

    • Chasing airspeed with big pitch changes; not trimming; no level-off lead; forgetting the power after level-off.

    ±10 kt · ±10° · level-off ±100 ft

    USAA p25–26 · power p8

    Constant-Rate Climbs & DescentsFL6·7Fly

    Procedure

    • VSI is primary for pitch. When airspeed is also specified, pitch sets the rate and power holds the airspeed.
    • 172 precision descent: 85 kt, 1800 RPM, −3°, −500 fpm. Climb: start from the cruise-climb attitude and adjust power for speed.
    • Level-off lead 10% of the VSI; trim.

    Teach

    • Small AI changes, then wait — the VSI lags. The ILS glideslope is the real-world version.

    Common errors

    • Chasing the VSI; using power for rate and pitch for speed in the wrong combination; no power adjustment.

    ±10 kt · ±10° · level-off ±100 ft

    USAA p29–30 · power p8

    Timed Turns to Compass HeadingsFL6·7Fly

    Procedure

    • Degrees to turn ÷ 3 = seconds at standard rate (30° = 10 s · 90° = 30 s · 180° = 1 min).
    • Roll into standard rate on the TC; start timing once it's established; hold the rate; roll out when time expires; read the compass wings-level.
    • Small changes: less than standard rate (half standard: degrees ÷ 1.5).
    • Calibrate the TC first — time a standard-rate turn each way against the clock.

    Teach

    • HI covered. Student states degrees, direction and time before rolling in. Use the Turn Timer.

    Common errors

    • Wrong arithmetic; starting the clock at roll-in; not holding standard rate; altitude loss on partial panel.

    ±100 ft · ±10 kt · rollout ±10°

    USAA p33–34

    Magnetic Compass TurnsFL6·7Fly

    Know

    • UNOS — Undershoot North, Overshoot South (turning error from magnetic dip). ANDS — Accelerate North, Decelerate South (on E/W headings; hold airspeed steady to read it).
    • Error ≈ latitude at N/S, 0 at E/W, in between: latitude × (degrees off E/W) ÷ 90. Dallas ≈ 33°N: 33 at 360/180 · 22 at 030/330/150/210 · 11 at 060/300/120/240 · 0 at 090/270.
    • North half: roll out early by error + ½ bank. South half: roll out late by error − ½ bank. E/W: ½-bank lead only.
    • At 110 kt (16° bank, 8° lead): right to 360 → start rollout at 319 · left to 360 → 041 · right to 180 → 205 · left to 180 → 155.
    • The card reads "backwards" — turn away from the number you want. Also oscillation (read the average) and deviation (check the card).

    Teach

    • Student calls it before rolling in: "North half, undershoot 33 plus 8 — roll out at 319." Drill with the calculator's Drill me button.

    Common errors

    • UNOS backwards; forgetting the bank lead; reading the compass while turning or accelerating.

    ±100 ft · ±10 kt · rollout ±10°

    USAA p35–36 (chart drawn for ≈30°N)

    Steep Turns (IR)FL6·7Fly

    Procedure

    • 45° bank, 180° or 360°, left then right; enter at or below VA (2300 RPM).
    • Passing ~30° of bank: add back pressure (AI pitch slightly up), add ~100–200 RPM, trim as required.
    • AI is primary for bank (the TC is past standard rate), ALT for pitch, VSI shows the trend.
    • Roll out about ½ the bank early (~20°); reduce power; release back pressure and take the trim out, or it balloons.
    • 45° = 1.41 G — stall speed up about 19%.

    Teach

    • If it's descending, shallow the bank first, then raise the nose, then re-establish the bank.

    Common errors

    • Not enough back pressure (altitude loss); overbanking tendency; ballooning on rollout; fixing altitude with bank alone.

    ±100 ft · ±10 kt · bank ±5° · rollout ±10°

    Recovery from Unusual AttitudesFL6·7Fly

    Setup

    • Student hands off, head down, eyes closed; you maneuver to disorient; "You have the flight controls."

    Nose high — airspeed decreasing

    • Full power → forward elevator to lower the pitch → level when the airspeed stops moving → level the wings with the TC.

    Nose low — airspeed increasing

    • Close the throttle → level the wings with the TC → gently pull out → level when the airspeed stops moving.

    Know

    • Pitch from the ASI and altimeter, bank from the TC — don't use the AI for pitch (it may have tumbled). The sequence depends on the attitude when the recovery starts.

    Teach

    • Recognize → say it ("nose low, airspeed increasing") → recover. Full panel first, then partial panel.

    Common errors

    • Pulling with the wings banked (tightens the spiral, over-G); wrong sequence; overcorrecting into the opposite attitude; fixating on the AI.

    Recognize nose-high and nose-low; recover in the correct sequence to stabilized level flight
