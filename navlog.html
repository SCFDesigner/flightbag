1 · THE SPEED CHAIN — IAS → CAS → TAS → GS

IAS+ position/instrument error → CAS+ altitude & temp → TAS± wind → GS

IAS → CAS. What the airspeed indicator reads vs. what it should read. Read it off POH Figure 5-1 (table below). On the 152 the error is largest slow — 40 KIAS is really 46 KCAS — and near zero around 60 KIAS.

CAS → TAS. Thinner air means the same dynamic pressure comes from a higher true speed. The rule of thumb is +2% per 1,000 ft, which is deliberately generous — the real figure at light-single altitudes is closer to +1.6% per 1,000 ft, so the rule runs a few knots fast by 8,000 ft. This page uses the exact form:

σ = (1 − 6.87535e-6 × PA)^5.2559 ÷ ((OAT+273.15) ÷ 288.15) TAS = CAS ÷ √σ

For cruise legs this page does not use that formula — it reads KTAS straight out of POH Figure 5-7, which already accounts for altitude and temperature. The formula is used for climb and descent legs, and shown as a cross-check.

TAS → GS. Wind triangle — section 2.

2 · WIND TRIANGLE — WCA AND GROUND SPEED

Wind direction on a nav log is true and is where the wind is coming from. Winds aloft forecasts are true; tower/ATIS winds are magnetic.

θ = wind dir − true course XW = wind vel × sin θ (+ = from the right) HW = wind vel × cos θ (+ = headwind) WCA = arcsin( XW ÷ TAS ) (+ = crab right) GS = TAS × cos(WCA) − HW

Turn into the wind: wind from the right gives a positive (right) WCA, which is added to true course. That is the same answer a whiz wheel gives, and it is exactly what your ASA calculator's wind function does.

Rough check: a direct headwind subtracts its full value from TAS; a direct crosswind barely changes GS at all.

3 · THE HEADING CHAIN — TC → TH → MH → CH

TC+ WCA → TH+ VAR → MH+ DEV → CH

True Virgins Make Dull Company — True, Virgins(Variation), Magnetic, Dull(Deviation), Compass. Add Whiskey going down (Add Westerly) — westerly variation and deviation are added.

  • TC — measured off the sectional against a meridian.
  • WCA — from the wind triangle. −Left / +Right.
  • VAR — isogonic line on the chart. −East / +West. "East is least, west is best."
  • DEV — from the compass correction card in the airplane, ± as placarded.

Going the other way (compass → true) you reverse every sign. That is where most people flip a variation and end up 20° off.

4 · ALTITUDES — PA, ISA AND DA

PA = indicated alt + (29.92 − altimeter) × 1000 ISA = 15 − 2 × (PA ÷ 1000) °C DA = PA + 118.8 × (OAT − ISA)

Every POH performance table on this page is entered with pressure altitude, never indicated. Density altitude is shown for situational awareness — it drives what the airplane actually feels like, but the 152 tables are indexed on PA and temperature separately.

ISA deviation (OAT − ISA) is what picks the column in the cruise table: 20°C below standard, standard, or 20°C above. This page interpolates between those columns rather than rounding to the nearest.

5 · CLIMB — POH FIGURE 5-6

The table is cumulative from sea level, so a climb is always a subtraction:

climb time = t(PA at TOC) − t(PA at field) climb fuel = f(PA at TOC) − f(PA at field) climb dist = d(PA at TOC) − d(PA at field)

Then the POH's own notes are applied:

  • + 0.8 gal for engine start, taxi and takeoff (added once, at the top of the fuel column — not per climb).
  • + 10% to time, fuel and distance for each 10 °C above standard. No credit is taken below standard; the POH does not offer one.
  • POH climb distances are zero wind. This page also computes a wind-corrected ground distance using average climb TAS through the wind triangle — that is the number carried into the nav log.

A step climb is the same subtraction run a second time between the two cruise altitudes.

6 · CRUISE — POH FIGURE 5-7

Enter with pressure altitude and RPM; read %BHP, KTAS and GPH. The table is published at 2,000 ft steps and at three temperatures. This page interpolates on all three axes — altitude, RPM and ISA deviation — and clamps at the table edges rather than extrapolating.

Where the table shows dashes (top RPM at cold temperatures) that power setting is not available; the page falls back to the highest RPM that has data and says so.

Table conditions: 1670 lb, recommended lean mixture, Lycoming O-235-L2C. KTAS assumes speed fairings, worth about 2 kt — take 2 kt off if the airplane doesn't have them.

7 · DESCENT — NOT POH DATA

The 152 POH publishes no descent table. Descent time, distance and fuel here come from the three fields you set on the Destination card — rate, IAS and GPH — not from the handbook:

time = (cruise alt − TPA alt) ÷ descent rate dist = time × GS at descent TAS fuel = time × descent GPH

Defaults are 500 fpm, 90 KIAS and 4.5 gph, which is a normal power-on cruise descent in a 152. Change them to match how you actually fly it.
