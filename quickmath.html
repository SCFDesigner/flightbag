Flight Bag

Quick Math

Rules of thumb for doing it by hand: on the written, in the oral, or when the calculator is in the other bag. Each card gives the formula, the shortcut, and one worked example. These are the standard approximations from the PHAK, Instrument Flying Handbook and AIM. Where a test wants chart precision, use the chart; use these to check that the chart answer makes sense.

Atmosphere & performance

Pressure altitudePHAK ch. 4

PA = field elevation + (29.92 − altimeter) × 1000

1 inHg ≈ 1000 ft. Altimeter lower than 29.92 → add; higher → subtract. Think "low pressure, high PA".

Example · Field 650 ft, altimeter 30.12
29.92 − 30.12 = −0.20 → −200 ft
650 − 200 = 450 ft PA

Density altitudePHAK ch. 11

DA ≈ PA + 120 × (OAT − ISA temp) ISA temp = 15 − 2 × (PA ÷ 1000)

+120 ft per °C warmer than standard. Standard temp drops 2 °C per 1000 ft from 15 °C at sea level.

Example · PA 2000 ft, OAT 30 °C
ISA at 2000 = 15 − 4 = 11 °C
30 − 11 = +19 °C → 19 × 120 = 2280
2000 + 2280 ≈ 4300 ft DA

Standard atmospherePHAK ch. 4

Sea levelLapse per 1000 ft
15 °C / 59 °F−2 °C / −3.5 °F
29.92 inHg / 1013.2 hPa−1 inHg (≈ −30 hPa)

Everything above is measured against this. ISA deviation = actual − standard at that altitude.

True airspeedPHAK ch. 8

TAS ≈ CAS + 2% per 1000 ft (of altitude)

Air thins with altitude, so the same indicated speed is a faster true speed. Use density altitude for the best estimate.

Example · 100 KCAS at 6000 ft
6 × 2% = 12% → 100 × 1.12 = 112 KTAS

Hydroplaning speedAIM 4-3-23 / PHAK ch. 11

V ≈ 9 × √(tire pressure, psi)

Example · tire at 36 psi
√36 = 6 → 9 × 6 = 54 kt

Weather

Cloud base (cumulus)PHAK ch. 12

Base AGL ≈ (Temp − Dew point) ÷ 2.5 × 1000 [°C] Base AGL ≈ (Temp − Dew point) ÷ 4.4 × 1000 [°F]

°C spread × 400 ft is the same thing and quicker. Unsaturated air cools 3 °C per 1000 ft while the dew point drops about 0.5 °C, so they meet at 2.5 °C per 1000 ft. For convective (fair-weather cumulus) bases only.

Example · METAR 28/16
spread 12 ÷ 2.5 = 4.8 → ≈ 4800 ft AGL

Freezing levelPHAK ch. 12 (2 °C lapse)

Freezing level AGL ≈ surface temp (°C) ÷ 2 × 1000

Assumes the standard 2 °C per 1000 ft. Real lapse rates vary; a Skew-T or the icing forecast beats this.

Example · surface 8 °C
8 ÷ 2 = 4 → ≈ 4000 ft AGL

°C ↔ °Fexact + shortcut

°F = °C × 9⁄5 + 32 quick: double it, add 30 °C = (°F − 32) × 5⁄9 quick: take away 30, halve it

The shortcut is within about 2° in the normal flying range. Anchors: −40 = −40 · 0 °C = 32 °F · 10 = 50 · 20 = 68 · 30 = 86 · 37 = 98.6.

Example · 25 °C
exact 25 × 1.8 + 32 = 77 °F · quick 50 + 30 = 80 °F

Minutes past the hour → fraction of the hour. Crosswind = wind × sin(angle), headwind = wind × cos(angle). Use the gust for the crosswind limit.

Example · RWY 18, wind 230 at 20
50° off → ~¾ × 20 ≈ 15 kt crosswind, ~13 kt headwind

Navigation & fuel

Time · speed · distancePHAK ch. 16

minutes = distance ÷ GS × 60 NM per minute = GS ÷ 60 distance = GS × minutes ÷ 60

Know your miles-per-minute: 90 kt = 1.5, 100 = 1.67, 120 = 2, 150 = 2.5. Then distance ÷ mpm is the time.

Example · 32 NM at 96 kt
96 ÷ 60 = 1.6 NM/min → 32 ÷ 1.6 = 20 min

FuelPHAK ch. 16

gallons = GPH × hours endurance (hr) = usable gal ÷ GPH avgas 6 lb/gal · Jet A 6.7 · oil 7.5

Day VFR reserve is 30 min, night 45, IFR 45 after the alternate (14 CFR 91.151 / 91.167). Plan with the POH cruise GPH plus start/taxi/climb.

Example · 24.5 gal usable, 6.1 GPH
24.5 ÷ 6.1 = 4.0 hr total → 3.5 hr with a 30-min reserve

1-in-60 ruleIFH ch. 9

1 NM off per 60 NM flown = 1° off course correction to parallel = off ÷ flown × 60 to rejoin at destination add: off ÷ remaining × 60

Example · 3 NM off after 45 NM, 90 NM to go
3 ÷ 45 × 60 = 4° to parallel
3 ÷ 90 × 60 = 2° more → turn 6° toward course

Reciprocal—

under 180: +200 then −20 180 and up: −200 then +20

040 → 240 − 20 = 220 · 310 → 110 + 20 = 130 · Drill it in Radial Math.

VOR time / distance to stationIFH ch. 9

time to station (min) = minutes flown × 60 ÷ degrees of bearing change distance (NM) = TAS × minutes flown ÷ degrees of bearing change

Fly perpendicular to the radial (needle off the wing tip), time a 10° bearing change, plug in.

Example · 10° took 2.5 min at 120 KTAS
2.5 × 60 ÷ 10 = 15 min to station
120 × 2.5 ÷ 10 = 30 NM

Instrument flying

Descent planning · 3°IFH ch. 10 / AIM 5-4-5

3° path = 300 ft per NM (≈ 318 exactly) NM to descend = altitude to lose ÷ 300 rate (fpm) for 3° = groundspeed × 5 any gradient: fpm = GS ÷ 60 × ft per NM

Three times the thousands: lose 6000 ft → start 18–20 NM out. At 90 kt GS a 3° path needs 450 fpm; at 120 kt, 600.

Example · 7000 → 2000 at 100 kt GS
5000 ÷ 300 ≈ 17 NM · 100 × 5 = 500 fpm

Visual descent pointIFH ch. 10

VDP distance (NM from threshold) = HAT ÷ 300 timing VDP: subtract 10% of HAT (in seconds) from the approach time

Example · MDA 1200, TDZE 600 → HAT 600
600 ÷ 300 = 2 NM from the threshold
timed: 60 s before the MAP

Standard rate & timed turnsIFH ch. 5 / USAA IR Guide p31–36

standard rate = 3°/s → seconds = degrees ÷ 3 bank for standard rate ≈ KIAS ÷ 10 + half of that roll-out lead ≈ half the bank angle

Example · 100 KIAS, turn 090 → 270
bank 10 + 5 = 15° · 180 ÷ 3 = 60 s · lead 7–8° · Compass errors (UNOS) are in Compass & Timed Turns.

DME arc leadIFH ch. 9

lead distance (NM) ≈ GS ÷ 200 (½% of GS) lead radial (°) = 60 × lead NM ÷ arc DME

Example · 120 kt GS, 10 DME arc
120 ÷ 200 = 0.6 NM → 60 × 0.6 ÷ 10 ≈ 4° lead · Fly one in the DME Arc Trainer.

Glideslope descent rateIFH ch. 10

fpm ≈ groundspeed × 5 (3° glideslope) or: half the groundspeed, add a zero

90 kt → 450 fpm · 110 kt → 550 fpm. Set it before glideslope intercept, then trim and bracket.
