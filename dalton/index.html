    Reconciling the sheet

    1. Why oil is a separate line. The 1971–72 172L lists a licensed empty weight, which does not include engine oil. The POH adds 8 qt (15 lb) on its own line. Its arm is −13.3 in because the oil sits ahead of the datum (front face of the firewall), so its moment is negative: 15 × −13.3 = −199.5 lb-in. It adds 15 lb but pulls the CG slightly forward. Tick “Oil already in empty weight” only if N814R’s W&B record says the empty weight includes full oil.
    2. The sheet mixes moment units. Oil is printed as −200, which is lb-in. Taxi fuel is printed as −0.3, which is moment/1000 (7 × 48 = 336 lb-in = 0.34). Adding −200 into a /1000 column, or −0.3 into a lb-in column, throws the total off. Pick one column and keep every line in it. The table shows both.
    3. The Base 2 box typo. “Mom/1000 39.3” is the empty-weight arm, not a moment. Moment = 1421.5 × 39.3 = 55,864.95 lb-in, or 55.86 in /1000.
    4. What /1000 is for. It only scales moments so they fit the envelope chart. Apply it to every moment (or just the totals), never to one line. For CG, use full lb-in: CG = total moment ÷ total weight.
    5. Fuel to destination. fuel burn (gal/hr) × 6 lb/gal × flight time (hr). Flight time takes decimal hours or h:mm. The 8 gal/hr default is a planning number; use the school’s figure if it differs.
    6. Arms. Everything except empty weight can be edited in the table’s Arm column. Front seats use the sheet’s 37 in; the real arm runs 34–46 in depending on seat position.
    7. Envelope. Redrawn from the 172L POH chart: normal category CG 35.0–47.3 in up to 1950 lb, forward limit moving to 38.5 in at 2300 lb; utility 35.0–40.5 in, max 2000 lb.

    For checking math only. The aircraft’s own W&B record and POH are the authority.
